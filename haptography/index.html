          A/B shares repeat and pause settings. Matching drive peaks does not guarantee equal felt intensity.

            Plug the recording device (the capture build) into this computer's USB and press Connect board: Chrome or Edge on a computer lists its serial port. Demo plays a simulated board instead.

            Name the dataset and the surface, then Record (or press R). Hold the device still for the first 4 s so the gyro's bias can be learned, then slide it toward and away from the wall the distance sensor points at, at different speeds.

            Lost counts packets that never arrived, Bad ones that arrived damaged, Dropped ones the board couldn't send. All three should stay at 0.

            Takes are kept in this browser only. Export writes each take as a session folder (raw.bin, CSVs, mic.wav, info.json and labels.json) in the layout the recording tools on the computer read; Import reads such folders back.

            Export writes the selected take; Export dataset every take of the dataset shown under Score, plus dataset.json with its scores.

            A take you untick under Score › Use stays in the library but doesn't count towards its dataset.

            Live shows the last 5 s from the board. Replay shows the selected take: scroll to zoom, Shift-scroll to pan, click to move the playhead, drag to select a stretch and label it as a span, double-click to fit.

            Vibration is the accelerometer with gravity and hand motion filtered out (above 5 Hz). Listening to it plays its strongest axis at its own pace: the texture as you would feel it, heard.

            Speed comes from the distance sensor alone, uncorrected for tilt; shaded bands are the strokes found in it (blue away from the wall, red toward it).

            Spectrogram draws the vibration (to 835 Hz; the accelerometer rolls off above ~400 Hz) and the sound (to 8 kHz, log scale) as frequency over time. Spectrum shows their power spectra: live, the last second; in a take, the strokes, the quiet between them, and a selection if you made one. Compare surfaces overlays every surface of the dataset.

            Grain: a texture with a spacing λ vibrates at speed ÷ λ, so its strokes line up along a line through zero; the dashed line is the surface's median grain.

            Each take scores out of 100: the serial link (15), the IMU's samples (10), the microphone (10), good distance readings (20), 4 s still at the start (10), strokes found (15), the texture signal (10: the strokes' vibration at least 10 dB over the quiet between them) and IMU headroom (10).

            Each surface in a dataset scores on its usable takes (scoring 60 or more and ticked Use): their mean score scaled by having 3 of them (40), 24 strokes (30) and 3 strokes in each mean-speed band (30). Under it: the grain (its texture's spacing, from speed ÷ vibration frequency), how alike its takes' vibration spectra are, and what to record next.
