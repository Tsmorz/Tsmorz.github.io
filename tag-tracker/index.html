Track AprilTags with your camera

Pick a camera and press Start. Show a printed tag36h11 tag to the camera, give it a name, and watch its last second of motion in 3D. Add more cameras to track from several sides.

Video is processed on this computer and never sent anywhere.

Recording

Records every pose the Track tab plots, with your notes.

Press V and speak. “Track”, “Calibrate”, “Stream”, “Settings”, “Start recording”, “Stop recording”, “Tare”, “Clear tare”, “Calibrate robot”, “Fit” and “Reset view” are commands; anything else is a note. Speech stays on this computer; the model downloads once (40 MB, or 120 MB on the graphics card).

Rigid objects

Fix two or more tags to one object, at different angles, and it is tracked as one pose from all their corners: steadier, and free of the mirror-image flips a single flat tag can have.

Tags

The first tag you pick is the object's origin. Then hold the object so that tag and each of the others are in view together, and turn it slowly while it learns how the tags sit.

Motion sensors

An nrf-controller board (XIAO nRF54LM20A Sense) fixed to a tag or an object sends its gyro and VQF orientation over Bluetooth, 208 times a second. Once it has learned how it sits, Filtered poses use it: steadier turns, and no mirror-image doubt even with one camera.

Stream

What is sent

One message for every track in view: its key (a tag's ID, or b: and an object's ID), its name, its position in metres and its orientation as a unit quaternion w, x, y, z. The frame is the 3D view's: the camera, or the tag chosen in Relative to, with any tare.

Set up

1. A camera

Tag Tracker finds AprilTags in your camera's picture and works out where each one is in 3D. Up to three tags drive a robot: one on your chest and one on each wrist. Video stays on this computer.

2. Your tags

How many tags? One drives one arm, two drive both arms, and a third on your chest adds the body. Use tag36h11 tags: print them, or show them on a phone.

Size is the black square. On a phone, measure it on the screen and enter that.

3. The lens (optional)

Distances are a guess until the camera is calibrated: show it a printed or on-screen board from a dozen angles and it learns its lens. A minute's work, and kept for next time. You can skip this and come back to the Calibrate tab later.

4. The start pose

Face the camera with your arms at your sides, elbows bent so your forearms point forward, palms facing each other. Hold still and press Calibrate robot above the 3D view (or press C, or say “calibrate robot”). From then on the robot follows you; press it again whenever it drifts.
